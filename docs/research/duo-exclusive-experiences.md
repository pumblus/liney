# Duo-exclusive experiences for Liney

Research for pumblus/liney#20 (map #17, Liney 1.1.0: iPhone Duo support). Question: beyond baseline adaptation, which iPhone Duo capabilities could serve Liney's writing, browsing, and photo flows, and what are the privacy implications for journal content on an outward-facing display?

Researched 2026-10-07 against Apple's developer site, HIG, UIKit/SwiftUI/AVFoundation reference, the Duo tech talks, Apple Newsroom, and Apple-staff answers on the Developer Forums. Proposals only; decisions belong to the user and need an MVP.md scope update before any new product surface.

## Answer in brief

- The only way for a third-party app to draw on both displays at once is a camera capture scene accessory, and Apple staff say camera use is the only supported dual-display case "at present". Liney has no in-app camera (MVP Out of Scope), so **Liney can never show journal content on the outer display while the device is open**. That is the main privacy result, and it removes the "outward-facing display" risk for scene accessories.
- The remaining Duo-specific capabilities that fit Liney are small: an **arrangement view for photo detail**, using the **hinge status to relock App Lock when the device is closed** (and to flush the editor), and, from the multiple-scenes layer, **a second Liney window on the inner display**.
- Hinge-angle effects, tent/laptop special layouts, outer-display companions, camera accessories, and Apple Pencil handwriting do not fit Liney's principles and are rejected below.
- Two privacy facts matter for the spec whatever is chosen: closing iPhone Duo is a resize, not a background transition, so **today's App Lock does not relock on close**; and **iPhone Duo uses Touch ID in the side button**, not Face ID.

## Platform facts

### What the device is

- Two displays: a 5.4-inch outer display and a 7.6-inch inner display; Touch ID in the side button "whether iPhone Duo is open or closed"; ships with iOS 27.1 ([Apple Newsroom, "Apple unveils iPhone Duo"](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/apple-unveils-iphone-duo/)).
- Poses Apple names: "partially folded, like a book, seated on a table like a laptop with the inner display facing you, or standing on its own, resting on its edges" ([Design for iPhone Duo, tech talk 111466](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111466/)). The HIG lists "partially folded like a book, placed down on a surface, or standing on its edges" ([HIG: Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)). The outer display "is a great opportunity to support landscape orientation, as people may want to set the phone down like a tent" ([Prepare your app for iPhone Duo, 111461](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111461/)).
- There is **no system pose value**. Apple staff: "There's no single system-provided pose value. Use the discrete hinge status ... and continuous hinge angle ... and infer the pose from angle, orientation, and size class together" ([iPhone Duo Group Lab summary, forum thread 847644](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847644)).
- Folding and unfolding is a geometry change, not a lifecycle event: "hinge angle changes are represented as size-class and trait updates, not a scene disconnect or app termination" (Apple staff, [thread 847644](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847644)). Opening and closing "works the same way" as resizing in iPhone Mirroring ([111461](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111461/)).
- Apple's own design principle: "Don't limit functionality to one pose or another. People may open and close the device frequently while using your app, so it needs to be predictable and consistent inside and out" ([111466](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111466/)); "You don't want to tie functionality to a specific pose" (same talk); HIG: "Maintain the same functionality across device poses" and "Avoid extreme layout changes as people fold the device" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)).

### Scene accessories in detail

- "A scene accessory declares supplementary content that the system presents on the app's behalf ... The app declares what content to provide; the system decides when and where to present it ... the app must remain fully functional without them" ([UISceneAccessory](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uisceneaccessory)).
- UIKit defines exactly two accessory kinds: `cameraCapture` and `externalNonInteractive` (same page, Topics).
- The camera capture accessory is presented only "while your app is in the foreground with an active capture session, while the capture interface runs on the inner display"; content "goes away when capture stops, when your app leaves the foreground, or when someone folds the device closed" ([Registering a camera capture accessory on iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/registering-a-camera-capture-accessory-on-iphone-duo)).
- Apple staff (Media Engineer) on [thread 847993](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847993), replying to a developer whose use case "is not photography": "Creating an AVCaptureSession and adding an input only is not supported ... You must show a camera preview on one or both of the displays", and "CameraCaptureAccessory is the only way to use both inner and outer displays at the same time. Camera cases are the only supported and intended use cases for dual display at present."
- Apple staff on [thread 847644](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847644): outer-display content while open is possible "for apps displaying camera previews or apps adopting AlarmKit"; non-camera apps can show content there "only via the system-controlled scene accessory mechanism — not by drawing on the outer display directly"; an app cannot make its accessory appear "independent of camera state".
- External displays changed in iOS 27: "your app receives a scene with the `windowExternalDisplayNonInteractive` role only after it registers a scene accessory. In earlier releases, the system connected this scene automatically" ([Presenting content on a connected display](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/presenting-content-on-a-connected-display)). Liney registers none, so a connected display mirrors the main scene, including the App Lock cover window.
- The outer display while open is otherwise used by system features only: "Duo FaceTime lets people who are with the user join a video call on the outer display" and "Kid Cue plays animations from Peanuts on the outer display" ([Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/apple-unveils-iphone-duo/)).

### Hinge observation in detail

- Hinge data "is observed live, and is ideal for driving interactions or effects. For layout, use the arrangement and region APIs" ([111464](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111464/)).
- "The rate and granularity of hinge angle updates are system policy and can change based on system state, so don't depend on a particular update frequency or precision" (Apple staff, [thread 847644](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847644)).
- The handler runs "when the hinge state changes, or when the interaction moves between hierarchies"; `hinge` is nil when the interaction leaves a hierarchy that provides hinge updates ([UIHingeInteraction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uihingeinteraction)).

### Arrangement views in detail

- "An arrangement view is a layout container that holds two views ... and dynamically organizes them based on display size, orientation, and reserved regions." A split arrangement "splits horizontally when the arrangement is wider than it is tall, and splits vertically when ... taller than it is wide"; an overlay arrangement layers the primary over the secondary and moves them to each side when partially folded ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)).
- Choosing: "A layout that places two views side by side or one above the other, such as an HStack or VStack, translates directly to a split arrangement" (HIG); use split for "a main-detail relationship ... It's important that neither of them are ever obscured", overlay for "a clear foreground/background relationship" ([111463](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111463/)).
- Constraints: "Keep navigation outside of arrangement views" (HIG); "avoid putting ArrangementViews inside of these scrollable containers" ([111463](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111463/)). Available "on all iOS devices running 27.1 and later" (Apple staff, [thread 847644](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847644)).
- A photo spread across a book's spine "no longer reads as one continuous image ... Content and controls that span the fold behave very similarly" ([111463](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111463/)). Continuous scrolling content such as "articles, feeds, documents, and lists don't displace" (same talk).

### First-party behaviour Apple documents

Nothing documented was found for the Journal app, Books, or Photos' viewer on iPhone Duo. What Apple does show:

- **Notes**: split view "adjusts the width of each pane to stay clearly visible as the device folds" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)); New Note should be among the last toolbar items to overflow ([111462](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111462/)).
- **Mail**: list or message when closed, both side by side when open (HIG).
- **Photos**: selecting a photo in an album moves the photo and its context menu together around the fold ([111463](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111463/)).
- **Podcasts**: Now Playing + transcript as a split arrangement; **Accessibility Reader** as an overlay arrangement; **Reminders** keeps an even 50/50 split; **Fitness** widens grid spacing at the hinge (all [111463](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111463/)).
- **Music** two-column layout, **Health** sidebar on the inner display, **Calculator** full width ([111466](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111466/), HIG).
- **Camera/FaceTime**: Kid Cue and Duo FaceTime on the outer display ([Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/apple-unveils-iphone-duo/)). **Clock**: alarm presentation across displays via AlarmKit and Live Activities ([thread 847644](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847644)).

## Privacy implications for Liney

1. **Outer display while open: closed to Liney by construction.** Without a running camera preview there is no supported way to present on the outer display while the inner display is in use ([thread 847993](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847993)). Liney's in-app camera is out of MVP scope, so no Liney journal content can face outward from an open device. Keep it that way: any proposal that registers a `UISceneAccessory` should be treated as a privacy change.
2. **Closing the device does not lock Liney today.** Closing is a size-class/trait update, not a scene disconnect ([thread 847644](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847644)). Liney's `AppLockModel` relocks only in `sceneDidEnterBackground` and covers on `sceneWillResignActive` (`Liney/LineyApp.swift:65-70`, `Liney/AppLock.swift:84-99`). Whether the scene also resigns active during the close transition is **unconfirmed**; no source says so, and Device Hub cannot show it reliably. Expected result: a user who closes the device mid-entry sees the entry continue on the outer display, unlocked. That matches Apple's continuity principle and Liney's current lock rules (no timeout options), but it is the opposite of closing a paper diary. See idea B.
3. **Tent pose.** Apple suggests people set the phone down "like a tent" with the app on the outer display in landscape ([111461](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111461/)). Which display faces whom in that pose, and whether the inner display is active, is **not documented**. It is a user choice to stand the device up with Liney open, as with any phone; Liney should not add tent-specific presentation.
4. **Every scene must be covered.** Duo is the first iPhone with multiple app instances, inner display only ([111464](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111464/)), and Liney's built plist already allows multiple scenes (#19 notes). Each scene has its own `AppLockModel` and cover window (`Liney/LineyApp.swift:36`, `Liney/AppLock.swift:129-162`), so two visible Liney windows mean two lock states. The baseline layer must decide this whatever happens to idea C.
5. **Touch ID, not Face ID.** iPhone Duo authenticates with Touch ID in the side button ([Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/apple-unveils-iphone-duo/)). `LocalAuthenticator` uses `.deviceOwnerAuthentication`, which covers both, and the Settings footer already says "Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode" (`Liney/SettingsViewController.swift:11`). MVP story 58 and App Store copy say "Face ID/passcode"; release notes and metadata for 1.1.0 should not imply Face ID on Duo.
6. **Hinge data is not personal data.** Hinge angle and status describe the device, not the journal. No source was found saying the hinge APIs need a privacy manifest entry (**unconfirmed**; not listed on the pages read).
7. **Accessory scenes are not a lock bypass if never registered.** `application(_:configurationForConnecting:options:)` returns the Journal configuration for every session role (`Liney/LineyApp.swift:25-30`). Since iOS 27 only registered accessories create accessory or external non-interactive scenes, this is safe on iOS 27. On iOS 17–26 the system connected the external non-interactive scene automatically and apps "opted out by declining to provide content for it" ([Presenting content on a connected display](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/presenting-content-on-a-connected-display)); Liney does not decline, so a cabled or AirPlay display on those versions may get a second, non-interactive journal scene with its own lock state (**unverified**, not Duo-specific; worth a separate issue). If Liney ever registers an accessory, the accessory's own `UISceneConfiguration` must not build journal UI, and its content must follow `hidesJournalContent`.

## Ranking by fit with Liney's principles

Principles used: local-only and private; calm, Apple-native, intentionally small; no speculative surface; same functionality in every pose (Apple's rule too); App Lock hides everything while locked.

| Rank | Candidate | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Photo detail as a split arrangement (A) | High | Existing screen, existing data, system container, no new surface; solves Apple's own "photo across the spine" problem. |
| 2 | Lock when closed, plus flush on close (B) | Medium-high | Privacy-forward and Duo-specific, but changes App Lock rules and works against Apple's continuity guidance; needs a user decision. |
| 3 | Second Liney window on the inner display (C) | Medium | Real journaling use (re-read while writing); belongs to the multiple-scenes layer; App Lock per scene must be solved first. |
| 4 | Fold-aware photo-group grid (even columns when a division region exists) | Low-medium, not exclusive | Already a hinge-aware layout question on map #17; Apple says scrolling content need not displace. Listed for cross-reference only. |
| — | Rejected (below) | Low | Hinge-angle effects, outer-display companion, camera capture accessory, tent/laptop special layouts, Apple Pencil, editor split of text and photos. |

## Proposed experience ideas

### A. Photo detail that opens like a book

Photo detail today is a vertical stack: the photo at 0.6 of the view height above a footnote with capture time and place, Done and a "Photo Actions" menu in the bar (`Liney/EntryEditorView.swift:713-749`). On iOS 27.1+ wrap the photo (primary) and its Photo Info (secondary: captured time, place name when present, Use as Entry Info, Delete Photo) in a `UIArrangementViewController` with `UISplitArrangement`, inside the existing navigation controller. On the outer display and on any portrait iPhone it looks as it does now. On the wide inner display the photo sits on one side and its info on the other; when the device is partially folded like a book, the system keeps each side within its region so the photo never spans the crease; in the laptop pose the photo sits above the fold and the actions below, matching Apple's "top for viewing, bottom for controls". It adds no data: the info pane shows exactly what photo detail shows today, and GPS-only coordinates stay out of the UI (MVP story 34). Below iOS 27.1 the current stack stays. Side note for the baseline layer: the HIG asks apps to "Reserve the ellipsis symbol for overflow, and give other menus a distinct symbol", which affects the `ellipsis.circle` Photo Actions item on Duo.

### B. Close the device, close the journal

When App Lock is enabled, add a `UIHingeInteraction` to each journal window (iOS 27.1+) and treat a transition to `UIHinge.Status.closed` like leaving the foreground: flush the open editor, then lock and cover, so reopening on either display asks for Touch ID. It reuses the existing lock screen and copy; nothing new is shown. Two variants for the user to choose between: always (closing a paper diary) or a Settings toggle "Lock When Closed" (a new preference, which MVP currently excludes with "no ... timeout setting"). Risks: Apple's guidance is that people open and close the device often and expect continuity, so this will interrupt someone who closes the phone to glance at it; status updates are system policy and may report `.unknown`; behaviour must be checked in Device Hub and recorded as a device deferral. Independently of the lock decision, flushing the editor on `.closed` is cheap insurance, because closing may not deactivate the scene (unconfirmed) and the editor today flushes on scene deactivation.

### C. Re-read while writing, in a second window

On the inner display, offer "Open in New Window" on timeline and search rows using `UIWindowSceneActivationAction`, which hides itself when new windows are unavailable (outer display, older iPhones) ([111464](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111464/)). A writer keeps last year's entry open on one half while writing today's on the other, using only system multitasking. This belongs to map #17's "Split View and multiple scenes" layer rather than a Duo-only API, and it also works on iPad. It needs a decision on shared App Lock state first: today each window locks and unlocks separately, so two windows can mean two Touch ID prompts or one window staying uncovered after App Lock is enabled in the other (#19 findings). Each window's editor already flushes on its own scene.

### (Not proposed) Do nothing extra

A valid choice: ship 1.1.0 with baseline, Split View, and hinge-aware layout only. The system containers Liney uses (navigation, split view, sheets, alerts, menus) already displace around the fold ([111466](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111466/)). Ideas A and B are small enough to add later without migration.

## Rejected candidates

- **Hinge-angle effects** (page-turn or zoom tied to the angle, "open the book" animations). Apple shows the angle for expressive interactions such as a whammy bar. For Liney it is decoration, adds custom motion to a calm app, and depends on update rates Apple says not to rely on.
- **Outer-display companion while open** (show a photo or entry to someone across the table, a "memory" slideshow). Not possible without a running camera preview ([thread 847993](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847993)); it would also put journal content on a display facing other people.
- **Camera capture accessory / in-app camera** (Kid Cue-style or a "selfie for today's entry"). Needs an in-app camera, which MVP lists as out of scope; adds camera permission and a capture session to a privacy-first journal; Simulator has no camera, so it cannot be verified under the simulator-only plan ([Registering a camera capture accessory](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/registering-a-camera-capture-accessory-on-iphone-duo)).
- **Tent or laptop special layouts** (a stand-up "reflection mode" or photo frame). There is no pose API; Apple warns against tying functionality to a pose; it would be new surface and would face content outward.
- **Apple Pencil handwriting.** Pencil support arrives "later this year" ([Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/apple-unveils-iphone-duo/)); Liney is plain text, and handwriting would mean a new block type and rich content, out of scope.
- **Splitting an entry's text and its photos across the fold.** Breaks the block order that defines an entry and puts an arrangement view inside a scrolling editor, which Apple advises against ([111463](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111463/)).
- **Inner-camera occlusion handling.** Liney never runs a camera, so the occlusion region never becomes active for it.

## Open questions and gaps

- Whether `sceneWillResignActive` fires during an open/close transition (determines whether Liney covers on close today). Not documented; needs Device Hub observation and a device check later.
- Which display is active, and which way it faces, in the tent pose. Not documented.
- Whether hinge status is delivered to scenes on the outer display when closed (the `closed` state) or only to the inner display. The docs say the update's `hinge` is nil outside "a hierarchy that provides hinge updates" without saying which hierarchies do.
- Whether Device Hub simulates hinge status and angle for `UIHingeInteraction`. Its controls "open, close, rotate, or fold" the device ([111461](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111461/)), but no source confirms hinge callbacks in Simulator.
- No Apple documentation was found on how Journal, Books, or the Photos viewer behave on iPhone Duo.
